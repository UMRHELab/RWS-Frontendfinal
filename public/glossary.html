Glossary

Definitions for the scientific terms and sensor names.

Data Explorer

Building / Room / Sensor

The three dropdowns that narrow down exactly which physical sensor you're looking at: pick the building first, then the room inside it, then the specific sensor in that room.

Data Type

Which measurement you want from the sensor you picked, for example radon concentration, temperature, or a radiation energy spectrum ("counts").

Time Range

How far back the chart and table should go: the last 1, 7, or 30 days.

Average

The mean of every value your query returned, shown once you click "Get Data."

Readings

How many individual data points your query returned in total.

Timestamp / Energy

The label on each table row: a date and time for most data types, or an energy level for the "counts" data type, which plots a radiation spectrum instead of a value over time.

Counts

For the "counts" data type, how many radiation particles were detected at a given energy level. This is what draws a spectrum chart instead of a line-over-time chart.

Export CSV

Downloads everything your last query returned as a spreadsheet-ready .csv file, not just the rows currently shown in the table.

Radiation & Radon

Background radiation

Radiation that's naturally present everywhere, from the ground, the air, space, and even the materials buildings are made of. The levels shown on this site are normal and not harmful.

Gamma radiation

A type of radiation counted by the Geiger tubes at our stations, reported as counts per minute.

Geiger tube / Geiger counter

The detector inside a radiation sensor that registers a "click" each time it's struck by a particle of radiation. More clicks per minute means a higher radiation level.

Counts per minute (cpm)

How many radiation particles a Geiger tube detects in one minute: the raw number a radiation sensor reports before it's converted into other units.

pCi/L (picocuries per liter)

The unit used to measure radon concentration in air. It tells you how many radioactive decays are happening per liter of air.

Radon

A naturally occurring radioactive gas that seeps up from the ground. It's colorless and odorless, which is why it has to be measured with a sensor rather than noticed directly.

RAD8

A continuous radon monitor used at some of our stations. It splits incoming radiation into hundreds of energy channels (its energy spectrum) before reporting a radon reading.

RadonEye

A compact radon detector used at some indoor stations to track radon concentration in pCi/L.

Sword / Gamon-D / Gamon-S

Other models of radiation detectors used across the RWS network. Like the RAD8, they report an energy spectrum that gets converted into a radiation reading using each device's own calibration.

EPA action level

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's recommended threshold for radon: 4 pCi/L. Above that, the EPA recommends taking action to reduce radon levels.

Normal / Elevated / High

The three status labels used on this site for radiation readings: Normal is below 2 pCi/L, Elevated is approaching the EPA action level, and High is above the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L.

Weather & Environment

Air temperature & humidity

The temperature of the outside air (°F) and how much water vapor is in it, shown as a percentage. Both are measured by a BME680 sensor at some stations.

Barometric pressure

Atmospheric pressure: the weight of the air pushing down at a station's location. Falling pressure often signals a change in weather is coming.

Wind speed & direction

How fast the wind is blowing (in mph) and which direction it's coming from, recorded by the rooftop weather logger.

Rainfall

How much rain has fallen, measured in inches.

Solar radiation

How much sunlight energy is hitting the rooftop sensor, used as a general measure of incoming sunlight intensity.

Soil moisture & temperature

How wet the ground is, and how warm it is a few inches below the surface. Together they help track ground conditions.

Datalogger

The on-site device that reads all the connected sensors at a station and logs a new set of measurements every few minutes.
